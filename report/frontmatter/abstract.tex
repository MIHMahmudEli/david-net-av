\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The purpose of this research is to address a critical limitation of current
synthetic-media forensics: existing audio-visual deepfake detectors reduce a
rich, multimodal problem to a single binary ``real versus fake'' decision,
discarding forensically essential information about \emph{which} modality of a
video was manipulated and \emph{when}. Because modern generative artificial
intelligence can synthesize facial video and cloned speech independently, a
clip may contain a genuine face with a cloned voice, a swapped face over
genuine speech, or a fully synthetic combination of both; distinguishing these
cases is necessary for journalism, content moderation, and legal contexts.

This thesis proposes a unified forensic framework that reformulates the task as
fine-grained, per-modality attribution. Given a video clip, the framework
independently determines whether the visual stream and the acoustic stream are
authentic or AI-generated, assigns the clip to one of four authenticity
quadrants, and temporally localizes the manipulated segments in each stream. At
its core is DAVID-Net, a disentangled dual-branch cross-modal transformer that
separates modality-specific authenticity evidence from cross-modal consistency
evidence, enabling the detection of internally consistent double-fakes that
defeat synchronization-based detectors. To generalize to unseen generators, the
study introduces Quadrant-Aware Contrastive Pretraining (QACP): a
self-supervised stage that constructs all four authenticity quadrants from
pristine data alone---using self-blended face video, vocoder copy-synthesis
audio, and cross-clip mismatch---and structures the embedding space with a
factorized supervised-contrastive objective, without using any deepfake
generator outputs. The framework is evaluated on FakeAVCeleb, AV-Deepfake1M,
and LAV-DF, with cross-dataset validation on DFDC and KoDF, using per-modality
AUC, four-class attribution accuracy, temporal localization average precision,
calibration, robustness, and fairness metrics.

On the FakeAVCeleb benchmark, the framework attains per-modality AUCs of
\dummy{0.986} (video) and \dummy{0.991} (audio) with
\dummy{94.6\%}~four-class attribution accuracy, matching specialized
unimodal detectors on both streams simultaneously. Under cross-dataset
evaluation on corpora never seen during training, QACP improves AUC by
\dummy{+0.065} absolute over the identical architecture without pretraining
(\dummy{0.838} on DFDC, \dummy{0.851} on KoDF) and by more than
\dummy{+0.10} over the strongest multimodal baseline, while
leave-one-generator-out AUC improves from \dummy{0.849} to \dummy{0.920},
confirming that an embedding space pretrained on synthetically constructed
quadrants generalizes to unseen generators better than supervised training
on generator outputs alone.

The findings would be helpful for forensic analysts, fact-checking
organizations, and online platforms that must not only flag manipulated media
but also explain which component was fabricated. The complete system is
released as reproducible open-source software and deployed as a public web
service comprising a FastAPI inference API and a Next.js user interface,
allowing end users to verify whether the video and the voice of a clip are
genuine or AI-generated.
