\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The rapid advancement of generative artificial intelligence has made it possible to
synthesize convincing fake video and cloned speech independently, producing
multimodal forgeries in which either stream---or both---may be manipulated. Existing
audio-visual deepfake detectors typically collapse this rich problem into a single
binary ``real versus fake'' decision, discarding forensically critical information
about \emph{which} modality was manipulated and \emph{when}. This thesis presents a
unified forensic framework that reformulates multimodal synthetic-media detection as
a fine-grained attribution problem: given a video clip, the framework independently
determines whether the visual stream and the acoustic stream are authentic or
AI-generated, assigns the clip to one of four authenticity quadrants (real/real,
real-video--fake-audio, fake-video--real-audio, fake/fake), and temporally localizes
the manipulated segments in each stream. At the core of the framework is
\textbf{DAVID-Net}, a disentangled dual-branch cross-modal transformer that separates
modality-specific authenticity evidence from cross-modal consistency evidence, allowing
it to detect internally consistent double-fakes that defeat synchronization-based
detectors. To achieve generalization to unseen generators, we introduce
\textbf{Quadrant-Aware Contrastive Pretraining (QACP)}, a self-supervised stage that
constructs all four authenticity quadrants from pristine data alone---via self-blended
face video, vocoder copy-synthesis audio, and cross-clip mismatch---and structures the
embedding space with a factorized supervised-contrastive objective, without using any
deepfake generator outputs. The framework is evaluated on FakeAVCeleb, AV-Deepfake1M,
and LAV-DF, with cross-dataset validation on DFDC and KoDF, using per-modality AUC,
four-class attribution accuracy, temporal localization AP, calibration, robustness,
and fairness metrics. \todo{Summarize final results here once experiments complete.}
The complete system is deployed as a public web service comprising a FastAPI inference
API and a Next.js user interface, enabling end users to verify whether the video and
voice of a clip are genuine or AI-generated.
