\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background Analysis}
\label{sec:background}

The rapid advancement of generative artificial intelligence has transformed the
creation of synthetic media from a specialized research activity into a
commodity capability. Face-swapping, facial reenactment, lip-synchronization
models, text-to-speech (TTS), and voice conversion (VC) systems are publicly
available, and their outputs are increasingly indistinguishable from genuine
recordings by human observers. International bodies and platform operators have
repeatedly warned that manipulated audio-visual content is now used for
disinformation, financial fraud, and non-consensual impersonation, making
reliable detection a societal necessity rather than a purely academic exercise.

The root cause of the detection problem is that the visual and acoustic streams
of a video can be manipulated \emph{independently}. A single clip can therefore
fall into one of four authenticity quadrants, summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:quadrants}: both streams real (RVRA), a cloned voice dubbed over
real footage (RVFA), a swapped or reenacted face over genuine speech (FVRA), or
a fully synthetic combination (FVFA).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{The four authenticity quadrants of a multimodal clip.}
\label{tab:quadrants}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
\textbf{Quadrant} & \textbf{Video} & \textbf{Audio} & \textbf{Typical origin} \\
\midrule
RVRA & Real & Real & Genuine recording \\
RVFA & Real & \textbf{Fake} & Voice clone / TTS dubbed over real footage \\
FVRA & \textbf{Fake} & Real & Face swap / reenactment over real speech \\
FVFA & \textbf{Fake} & \textbf{Fake} & Fully synthetic avatar (lip-sync + TTS) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

This problem is important because the overwhelming majority of published
detectors collapse it into a single binary decision. Such a formulation is
forensically inadequate for three reasons. First, it provides no
\emph{attribution}: a journalist, court, or moderation system needs to know
whether the face was swapped, the voice was cloned, or both. Second, binary
detectors tend to shortcut on whichever modality is easier to classify,
degrading accuracy on the harder stream. Third, modern manipulations are
increasingly \emph{partial}---only a few seconds of audio or a short facial
segment are altered---which a clip-level binary label cannot express. Solving
per-modality attribution with temporal localization therefore places this
thesis in the correct context of the wider synthetic-media forensics problem
domain.

\section{Existing Studies}
\label{sec:existing}

Five research threads are directly relevant to this study.

\textbf{Unimodal video detection.} Early detectors treated the task as
frame-level image classification: XceptionNet trained on
FaceForensics++~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics} became the canonical baseline,
followed by EfficientNet ensembles that won the Deepfake Detection
Challenge~\cite{dolhansky2020dfdc}. Temporal methods such as
LipForensics~\cite{haliassos2021lipforensics} and
RealForensics~\cite{haliassos2022realforensics} model mouth-motion dynamics for
stronger cross-manipulation generalization. These methods are blind to audio
manipulation and degrade sharply under compression and dataset shift.

\textbf{Unimodal audio spoofing detection.} The anti-spoofing community,
organized around the ASVspoof challenges~\cite{wang2020asvspoof}, progressed
from spectral features to end-to-end models such as
RawNet2~\cite{tak2021rawnet2} and the spectro-temporal graph attention network
AASIST~\cite{jung2022aasist}; self-supervised speech front-ends such as
WavLM~\cite{chen2022wavlm} further improved robustness. Audio-only methods
cannot detect facial manipulation.

\textbf{Audio-visual binary detection.} Multimodal detectors fuse both streams
but almost universally emit one label: the Modality Dissonance
Score~\cite{chugh2020mds}, affective-cue matching~\cite{mittal2020emotions},
and transformer fusion models such as AVFakeNet~\cite{ilyas2023avfakenet} and
AVoiD-DF~\cite{yang2023avoiddf}. A real-video/fake-audio clip and a
fake-video/real-audio clip receive the same output.

\textbf{Consistency and localization.} SyncNet~\cite{chung2016syncnet}-style
synchronization was exploited for anomaly-based detection~\cite{feng2023avanomaly},
but consistency methods detect \emph{mismatch} without attributing which stream
is fake and fail on fully synthetic clips whose fake streams are mutually
consistent. LAV-DF~\cite{cai2022lavdf} and AV-Deepfake1M~\cite{cai2023avdeepfake1m}
introduced temporal forgery localization, but localization is usually joint
over modalities and cross-dataset generalization is under-reported.

\textbf{Proxy-forgery training.} Self-Blended Images~\cite{shiohara2022sbi} and
Face X-ray~\cite{li2020facexray} showed that synthesizing blending artifacts
from pristine images generalizes better than training on generator outputs; the
anti-spoofing literature found the same for vocoder
copy-synthesis~\cite{wang2023vocoded}. Zhou and Lim~\cite{zhou2021joint}
explored joint audio-visual labels but trained on generator outputs without a
pretraining stage.

\textbf{Research gap.} As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:gap}, no existing method
simultaneously provides (a) independent per-modality authenticity, (b)
cross-modal consistency reasoning, (c) temporal localization, and (d) validated
cross-generator generalization in a single reproducible, deployable system.
This study utilizes the strongest components of the existing literature
(self-supervised encoders, contrastive synchronization, proxy forgeries) and
extends them to close exactly this gap.

\section{Research Motivation and Objective}
\label{sec:motivation}

The general aim of this thesis is to contribute a forensic framework that moves
synthetic-media detection from coarse binary classification to fine-grained,
per-modality attribution with temporal localization, and to demonstrate that
such attribution can generalize to generators never seen during training.

The specific objectives are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item to design a unified framework that detects, per modality, whether the
        video and the audio of a clip are real or AI-generated;
  \item to propose DAVID-Net, a disentangled cross-modal transformer with
        independent per-modality decision heads, a synchronization-consistency
        module, and per-modality temporal localization;
  \item to propose Quadrant-Aware Contrastive Pretraining (QACP), a
        generator-free pretraining strategy for cross-generator generalization;
  \item to evaluate the framework across multiple benchmarks with
        cross-dataset, robustness, calibration, and fairness protocols; and
  \item to deploy the detector as a publicly accessible web application.
\end{itemize}

The following research questions (RQs) are answered through the findings of
this research:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.}]
  \item Can a single model produce independent, well-calibrated authenticity
        decisions for the visual and acoustic streams while remaining
        competitive with specialized unimodal detectors?
  \item Does explicit modeling of audio-visual synchronization improve
        detection of manipulations that break cross-modal coherence?
  \item Does disentangling modality-specific authenticity evidence from
        cross-modal consistency evidence improve generalization to unseen
        generators and datasets?
  \item Can the model temporally localize which segments of each stream were
        manipulated, and does this objective aid the clip-level decision?
  \item Is the detector robust to real-world degradations and fair across
        demographic subgroups?
  \item Can an embedding space pretrained on synthetically constructed
        quadrants built from pristine data alone generalize to real forgeries
        from unseen generators better than supervised training on generator
        outputs?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Contribution}
\label{sec:contribution}

The target users of the proposed solution are forensic analysts, journalists
and fact-checking organizations, online platform moderation systems, and
ordinary end users who need to verify received media. They benefit from
verdicts that state \emph{which} stream was manipulated and \emph{when}, with
calibrated confidence, rather than an unexplained binary flag; the deployed web
interface makes this capability directly accessible without technical
expertise.

The contribution of this thesis to the development of scientific concepts is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{QACP}: a self-supervised pretraining stage that constructs all
        four authenticity quadrants from pristine data only and structures
        three embedding spaces with a factorized supervised-contrastive
        objective, including an explicit \emph{real-but-mismatched} class that
        teaches the model that desynchronization is not forgery;
  \item a reformulation of audio-visual deepfake detection as a multi-task,
        per-modality attribution and localization problem;
  \item \textbf{DAVID-Net}, a disentangled dual-branch cross-modal transformer
        architecture implementing this reformulation;
  \item a generalization-first evaluation protocol with leave-one-generator-out
        and cross-dataset testing as first-class results; and
  \item a complete, reproducible, publicly deployed detection system.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows.
Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} presents the research methodology, including the
conceptual framework (DAVID-Net and QACP), the data collection process, ethical
considerations, and the economic analysis. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the
results, their verification, and an impact analysis. The final chapter
concludes the thesis and outlines directions for future work.
