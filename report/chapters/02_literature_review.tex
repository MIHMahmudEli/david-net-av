\chapter{Literature Review}
\label{ch:literature}

This chapter surveys the five research threads most relevant to multimodal
synthetic-media forensics and identifies the gap this thesis addresses.

\section{Unimodal Video Deepfake Detection}
Early detectors treated deepfake detection as frame-level image classification.
XceptionNet trained on FaceForensics++~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics} became the
canonical baseline, followed by EfficientNet ensembles that won the Deepfake
Detection Challenge~\cite{dolhansky2020dfdc}. Frequency-domain methods exploit
GAN upsampling fingerprints, while temporal methods such as
LipForensics~\cite{haliassos2021lipforensics} and
RealForensics~\cite{haliassos2022realforensics} model mouth-motion dynamics for
stronger cross-manipulation generalization. \emph{Limitation:} these methods are
blind to audio manipulation and degrade sharply under compression and dataset
shift.

\section{Unimodal Audio Spoofing Detection}
The anti-spoofing community, organized around the ASVspoof
challenges~\cite{wang2020asvspoof}, progressed from hand-crafted spectral features
to end-to-end models such as RawNet2~\cite{tak2021rawnet2} and the spectro-temporal
graph attention network AASIST~\cite{jung2022aasist}. Self-supervised speech
front-ends such as WavLM~\cite{chen2022wavlm} further improved robustness to unseen
TTS and VC systems. \emph{Limitation:} audio-only methods cannot detect facial
manipulation.

\section{Audio-Visual Binary Detection}
Multimodal detectors fuse both streams but almost universally emit one binary
label. The Modality Dissonance Score~\cite{chugh2020mds} measures audio-visual
dissimilarity; Mittal et al.~\cite{mittal2020emotions} compare cross-modal
emotional cues; AVFakeNet~\cite{ilyas2023avfakenet} and
AVoiD-DF~\cite{yang2023avoiddf} employ transformer fusion. \emph{Limitation:} no
per-modality attribution; a real-video/fake-audio clip and a fake-video/real-audio
clip receive the same label.

\section{Audio-Visual Consistency and Synchronization}
SyncNet~\cite{chung2016syncnet} introduced contrastive audio-visual
synchronization, later exploited for anomaly-based deepfake detection
(AVAD~\cite{feng2023avanomaly}). \emph{Limitation:} consistency methods detect
\emph{mismatch} but cannot say which stream is fake, and they fail on
fully-synthetic clips whose fake streams are mutually consistent (e.g., a
lip-synced avatar with cloned voice).

\section{Temporal Localization of Partial Forgeries}
LAV-DF~\cite{cai2022lavdf} introduced content-driven partial forgeries with
boundary annotations and the BA-TFD detector; AV-Deepfake1M~\cite{cai2023avdeepfake1m}
scaled this to over one million clips. \emph{Limitation:} localization is usually
joint over modalities, per-modality attribution is limited, and cross-dataset
generalization is under-reported.

\section{Proxy-Forgery and Generator-Free Training}
Self-Blended Images~\cite{shiohara2022sbi} and Face X-ray~\cite{li2020facexray}
showed that \emph{synthesizing} blending artifacts from pristine images
generalizes better than training on generator outputs; the anti-spoofing
literature found the same for vocoder copy-synthesis
augmentation~\cite{wang2023vocoded}. Zhou and Lim~\cite{zhou2021joint} explored
joint audio-visual labels but trained on generator outputs without a pretraining
stage. \emph{Gap:} no prior work unifies the two unimodal proxy-forgery techniques
into a quadrant-structured audio-visual pretraining objective, nor introduces a
real-but-mismatched class as an explicit disentanglement signal.

\section{Summary of the Research Gap}
Table~\ref{tab:gap} summarizes the identified gap. No widely adopted method
simultaneously provides (a) independent per-modality authenticity, (b) cross-modal
consistency reasoning, (c) temporal localization, and (d) validated
cross-generator generalization in a single reproducible, deployable system. This
thesis targets exactly this intersection.
