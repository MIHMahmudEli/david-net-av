\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

The proposed solution answers the research questions through six families of
evidence: in-domain attribution (RQ1), cross-dataset and cross-generator
generalization (RQ3, RQ6), temporal localization (RQ4), module ablations
(RQ2, RQ3, RQ6), missing-modality operation (RQ1, RQ5), and robustness,
calibration, and fairness audits (RQ5). Unless stated otherwise, every
headline number is the mean $\pm$ standard deviation over three seeds
$\{42, 43, 44\}$ on the committed subject-disjoint test split, and all
baselines are evaluated under the identical split and preprocessing.

\subsection{In-Domain Detection and Attribution (RQ1)}
\label{sec:res-indomain}

Table~\ref{tab:indomain} reports per-modality detection and quadrant
attribution on the FakeAVCeleb subject-disjoint test split. Three
observations follow. First, the proposed framework produces \emph{both}
per-modality decisions at a quality that matches or exceeds the strongest
specialized unimodal detector on each stream: video AUC
\dummy{0.986\,$\pm$\,0.003} versus \dummy{0.934} for LipForensics, and audio
AUC \dummy{0.991\,$\pm$\,0.002} versus \dummy{0.953} for AASIST. A single
network therefore does not have to trade one modality against the other,
answering RQ1 affirmatively. Second, binary fusion baselines (MDS, AVoiD-DF,
AVFF) reach competitive \emph{clip-level} accuracy but are structurally
unable to attribute the manipulation to a stream; their column entries are
single any-fake scores. Third, quadrant attribution reaches
\dummy{94.6\,$\pm$\,0.5}\% four-class accuracy; the confusion matrix
(Figure~\ref{fig:confusion}) shows that residual errors concentrate in the
FVFA$\rightarrow$FVRA cell, i.e.\ fully synthetic clips whose cloned audio
is close to the real voice---the hardest attribution case, not a systematic
failure of the factorization. Per-modality ROC curves are shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:roc}.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset Generalization (RQ3, RQ6)}
\label{sec:res-crossdataset}

Table~\ref{tab:crossdataset} applies the FakeAVCeleb-trained model, without
any adaptation, to six external corpora that differ in generator, codec,
recording conditions and modality. Three of them are audio-visual
(Celeb-DF~v2, DFDC, DeepfakeTIMIT) and three are audio-only (In-the-Wild,
ASVspoof~2019~LA, WaveFake); audio-only inputs pass through the null-video
token path of Section~\ref{sec:davidnet}, so the audio verdict is
computed exactly as it would be for a voice note. The per-stream AUC is
reported wherever the corpus contains both classes for that stream; corpora
with a single class (all-fake WaveFake, all-fake DeepfakeTIMIT) contribute a
detection rate instead. Every method degrades under distribution shift; the
question is by how much. The video head retains an AUC of
\dummy{0.83\,$\pm$\,0.01} on Celeb-DF~v2 and the audio head
\dummy{0.86\,$\pm$\,0.01} on In-the-Wild---the two corpora that are furthest
from FakeAVCeleb in generator and acquisition---while the
architecture trained without QACP (ablation row of Table~\ref{tab:ablation})
loses \dummy{0.06} and \dummy{0.05} AUC respectively. Because the only
difference between the two is the pretraining stage, the gap isolates the
contribution of the generator-free quadrant construction and supports the
RQ6 hypothesis that pristine-data pretraining generalizes better than
supervised exposure to generator outputs alone. Figure~\ref{fig:roc-cross}
shows the corresponding ROC curves.

\subsection{Cross-Generator Generalization: Leave-One-Generator-Out (RQ6)}
\label{sec:res-logo}

The LOGO protocol removes every clip produced with one manipulation
\emph{family} from training and tests exclusively on it
(Table~\ref{tab:logo}). Families rather than individual tools are held out
because FakeAVCeleb contains hybrids: holding out Wav2Lip alone while
training on FSGAN+Wav2Lip clips would leak the held-out tool's lip-sync
artifacts. Test clips are further restricted to the subject-disjoint test
identities, so each LOGO run is simultaneously generator- and
subject-disjoint. The held-out-family AUC of the affected stream averages
\dummy{0.90}; the largest drop occurs for the Wav2Lip family
(\dummy{0.86}): lip-sync forgeries leave the smallest spatial footprint, so a
detector must rely on synchronization and consistency cues rather than
generator-specific texture. This is precisely the failure mode QACP is
designed to remove, since its self-blended and mismatch constructions teach
boundary and coherence cues that are property-level rather than
generator-level. Figure~\ref{fig:per-generator} complements the hold-out
analysis with the per-generator accuracy of the main model on the in-domain
test split.

\subsection{Ablation Study (RQ2, RQ3, RQ6)}
\label{sec:res-ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} removes one design element at a time;
Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} visualizes the same data. The central pattern is
that \emph{in-domain accuracy is nearly insensitive to the design choices,
while cross-dataset accuracy is highly sensitive}---confirming that the
in-domain benchmark is saturated and that architectural claims must be
defended out of domain. Specifically: removing QACP costs
\dummy{0.066} cross-dataset AUC but only \dummy{0.007} in domain; removing
only the MISMATCH pseudo-class costs \dummy{0.033} cross-dataset,
demonstrating that teaching ``desync $\neq$ fake'' matters beyond the
intuition (RQ6); removing the synchronization module costs \dummy{0.047}
cross-dataset and disproportionately harms lip-sync forgeries (RQ2);
removing the disentanglement penalty costs \dummy{0.029}, validating the
factorized design (RQ3); and collapsing the architecture to early or late
fusion---the standard designs in prior work---costs \dummy{0.104} and
\dummy{0.089} respectively. The multi-task formulation itself contributes:
a single-task binary variant loses \dummy{0.096} cross-dataset, and removing
the localization head alone costs \dummy{0.011}, indicating that the
frame-level objective acts as a spatially grounded regularizer for the
clip-level decision (RQ4).

\subsection{Temporal Localization (RQ4)}
\label{sec:res-localization}

FakeAVCeleb manipulations are whole-clip, so the corpus supports a
qualitative rather than an interval-level (AP@IoU) assessment of the
localization heads; the segment-annotated corpora that would enable the
latter (AV-Deepfake1M, LAV-DF) exceeded the storage budget of the training
environment and are left for future work (see the Conclusion).
Two properties nevertheless distinguish the localization heads from the
published localizers: the timeline is predicted \emph{per modality}, and
the same network simultaneously provides the clip-level attribution of
Section~\ref{sec:res-indomain}. A qualitative example is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:localization}: the per-frame probability is uniformly high
across a whole-clip manipulation and returns to baseline on the pristine
stream, and Figure~\ref{fig:explain} pairs the same timelines with the
input attributions and the synchronization-agreement curve of the sync
module.

\section{Accuracy and Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

The accuracy of the findings is verified through four mechanisms, each
establishing a direct connection between the collected research data and the
research objective.

First, \emph{statistical verification}: every headline metric is reported as
the mean and standard deviation over three seeds with bootstrap 95\%
confidence intervals (1{,}000 resamples). The cross-dataset AUC advantage of
the full framework over the strongest multimodal baseline is
\dummy{+0.104} on DFDC with a bootstrap 95\% CI of
\dummy{[+0.081, +0.126]}, and the corresponding DeLong
test~\cite{delong1988} rejects equality of the AUCs at
\dummy{$p < 0.001$} under Holm--Bonferroni correction over all baseline
comparisons. The QACP-versus-no-QACP contrast, the core scientific claim,
is significant at \dummy{$p < 0.001$} on both DFDC and KoDF, and its effect
size exceeds the three-seed standard deviation by an order of magnitude,
ruling out seed luck as an explanation.

Second, \emph{calibration}: expected calibration error and reliability
diagrams (Section~\ref{sec:res-calibration}) verify that reported
confidences are trustworthy, which is essential for forensic use.

Third, \emph{robustness}: detection quality is measured under compression,
blur, resolution loss, and audio noise
(Section~\ref{sec:res-robustness}) to identify potential points of failure,
and the observed degradation profile defines the operating envelope of the
system, which is documented in the deployed model card.

Fourth, \emph{external validity and provenance}: cross-dataset and
leave-one-generator-out protocols verify resilience to distribution shift
rather than fit to one benchmark, and every reported number traces to a
committed configuration file, split definition, and result JSON in the
project repository, so any figure or table entry can be regenerated with a
single command. Trade-offs between the full research model and the
lightweight deployed variant (Section~\ref{sec:res-efficiency}) are
presented to reflect realistic product requirements.

\section{Impact Analysis}
\label{sec:impact}

\textbf{Societal impact.} Reliable per-modality attribution strengthens the
information ecosystem: fact-checkers can state precisely which component of a
viral clip was fabricated, platforms can apply proportionate moderation, and
citizens gain a free verification tool. Conversely, detector outputs could be
misused to dismiss authentic evidence (``liar's dividend''); the system
mitigates this by reporting calibrated probabilities with explicit uncertainty
rather than categorical verdicts. The in-the-wild degradation documented by
Deepfake-Eval-2024~\cite{chandra2025deepfakeeval} underscores why the
cross-dataset numbers of Section~\ref{sec:res-crossdataset}, not the
in-domain numbers, should be read as the realistic performance expectation.

\textbf{Legal and safety issues.} Detection outputs are decision support, not
legal proof; the interface and model card state this explicitly. The
framework's localization output supports human expert review, which remains
the appropriate standard in legal contexts, and aligns with emerging
regulatory obligations for synthetic-media transparency such as the EU AI
Act~\cite{euaiact2024}.

\textbf{Cultural and fairness issues.} Because training corpora
over-represent some demographics and languages, the fairness audit
(Section~\ref{sec:res-fairness}) quantifies subgroup gaps, and balanced
sampling reduces them; residual gaps are documented so downstream users
understand for whom the detector is less reliable.

\textbf{Sustainability.} The cached-feature training regime and the
lightweight deployed variant minimize energy consumption relative to
end-to-end training and serving of large models. The QACP construction is
self-refreshing---new pseudo-forgeries can be synthesized from pristine data
as generators evolve---so the solution remains maintainable without repeated
large-scale data collection, benefiting stakeholders from platform operators
to end users.
